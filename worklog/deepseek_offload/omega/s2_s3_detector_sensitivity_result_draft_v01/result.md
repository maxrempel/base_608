**OMEGA Detector Sensitivity Pilot Result**

We assessed OMEGA detection sensitivity using deterministic synthetic non-reference payloads (120 bp) spiked into four samples (focal S2, focal S3, and two cultured controls) at three tiers of breakpoint-support reads (8+8, 12+12, 20+20 per side). A single outcome-blind, high-quality chromosome 22 locus, novel to reference databases (gnomAD-SV v4.1, HPRC v1.1), was selected under frozen gates (clip ≥30, cluster window 20, ≥5 clip records, clean mask, ≥8 per side, canonical two-sided junction, rare threshold 0.001). All 12 spike-in samples passed extraction, two-sided clustering, clean-mask, and assembled contig generation. However, zero of 12 spikes (0/4 per tier) passed the canonical two-sided junction gate; every assembled contig followed the reference haplotype without a qualifying two-sided foreign overhang. No canonical two-sided false-positive loci were observed in four sham controls, and no off-target canonical two-sided calls occurred across spike runs.

This result provides direct evidence of a detector sensitivity failure mode at the assembly-to-canonical junction stage. Consequently, low observed counts in focal S2/S3 Omun calls may partly reflect technical under-detection rather than biological absence. We do not claim that all low counts are explained, do not apply any correction factor, and refrain from biological interpretation of the cultured comparison.

**Limitations** include testing at only one locus, one payload length and topology, synthetic high-quality reads, correlated support tiers, and the possibility that real insertions assemble differently. **Recommendation**: The smallest next falsification is to repeat at several outcome-blind loci and payload lengths, incorporating a validated real-read positive control, before any burden interpretation is undertaken.
