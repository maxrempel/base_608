**The Disclosure Day Chronicles: Beyond the Bermuda Triangle**  
by J. F. Pleasants

**Summary for Max Rempel**

*The Disclosure Day Chronicles: Beyond the Bermuda Triangle*, published in 2026, is a young‑adult novel that blends family drama, adolescence, and a sprawling cosmic mythology. Set largely in the Florida Keys, the story follows fourteen‑year‑old Solara Ray Auñón—Sola—as she discovers she is a pivotal figure in a plan to raise Earth’s vibration for an event called Disclosure Day. The book’s events are presented in a fast‑paced sequence that intertwines Sola’s personal growth with galactic politics involving the Pleiadian‑led Galactic Federation of Worlds and its adversaries, the reptilian Ciakahrr Empire.

**A Girl, a Friend, and the Signs**

Sola lives with her protective father, Rick, on a boat in Key Haven, Florida. Her mother, Alana, struggles with depression and addiction and resides in a long‑term rehab facility. Sola’s closest companion is her pragmatic, germ‑conscious best friend, Blu. The two navigate the social cruelties of Coral Sands High School, where Sola, with her unusual sunset‑red hair, cacao‑toned skin, and ocean‑blue eyes, is a target for ridicule. Her grandmother, Astria—whom she calls Yaya—is a spiritual anchor who speaks to trees, fairies, and beings from other realms, and practices Quantum Healing Hypnosis Technique. Yaya has always told Sola that her unusual appearance and her ability to see “imaginary” hybrid children mean something far beyond genetics. Sola repeatedly sees the number 444, which Yaya and Blu’s mother, Rainbow Skye, call her angel number, a sign she is on the right path.

The story’s initial catalyst is a school assembly featuring retired British astronomer Captain Adam MacMillan, known as Cap. Sola and Blu had just been discussing aliens, and the assembly’s focus on the Pleiades star system strikes Sola as synchronicity. Cap invites students to a night stargazing session at the Southernmost Point. Sola persuades a reluctant Blu to go, and Yaya agrees to pick them up, reconnecting with Cap, her old high‑school friend.

Through Cap’s telescope, Sola looks at the Pleiades and experiences a visceral, personal connection. One star appears to pulse in rhythm with her heartbeat, and she feels a telepathic whisper of recognition. Immediately afterward, the group witnesses a UFO—a craft with icy‑blue lights dropping out of the sky near Wisteria Island. As they react, Yaya smiles calmly and whispers, “She’s here.”

**Vera Ora and the Galactic Mission**

The “she” is Lieutenant Vera Ora of the Galactic Federation of Worlds. Vera, a Pleiadian from the planet Errahel, has been sent on a perilous mission: to travel through Orion’s Portal (a heavily guarded gateway controlled by the Draconians) to Earth, known to the galaxy as the Blue Pearl, to prepare a human for the first phase of Disclosure Day. That human is Sola.

The Galactic Federation of Worlds is a peacekeeping coalition of star nations, guided by a council that includes Ashtar (Kai El). It works alongside the Confederation of Planets, a more subtle collective of mentors. The Federation has long protected Earth under a quarantine, but now the Council of Twenty‑Four, operating from an even higher density, has decreed that the veil must lift. Phase One is “activation”: raising one of three critical energy nodes on Earth’s planetary grid.

Vera receives her orders from Kai El in the Governance Chamber on Errahel. She is to oversee Solara Ray Auñón and, critically, must never make eye contact with her, as that would destabilize the timeline. Vera is also told that Sola must choose to assist of her own free will. The mission is fraught with danger, not least because the Ciakahrr Empire, a service‑to‑self reptilian race led by Master Nyxar and his lieutenant Korvus, monitors the Portal and opposes the Federation’s plans.

In parallel, Vera’s boyfriend, Lieutenant Atlas Arzo, an Adarian with partial Reptilian ancestry, insists on following her to Earth despite orders. He eventually travels through the Portal in an old Adarian ship, evading Draconian detection. His arrival, however, inadvertently allows the Ciakahrr to trace his energy signature to Earth, escalating the threat.

**First Contact and Abduction**

After the UFO sighting, Sola’s group—Cap, Blu, Astria, and Cosmo the labradoodle—sail to Wisteria Island to investigate. There they encounter a woman in oversized sunglasses and clothes who behaves oddly and tries to dissuade them. The island is swarming with iguanas that attack the woman, and Sola instinctively defends her with a driftwood branch, discharging a burst of static electricity. The woman is Vera, who uses a device to stun the reptiles.

Vera reveals her identity and explains that Sola has been chosen to help raise Earth’s vibration for Disclosure Day. Sola is overwhelmed but receives a telepathic warning to flee to the Dry Tortugas to escape the Draconians, who have tracked Vera. The group, after a tense debate, heads south. They are pursued by Draconian‑controlled iguanas and later by two Nagaai warriors (servants of the Ciakahrr) who have shapeshifted to look like park rangers. During the chaos at Fort Jefferson, Sola is seized by the Nagaai and taken aboard their ship, hurtling through space toward the Draconian homeworld.

**Sola’s Ordeal and Awakening**

The Nagaai’s ship is intercepted by a Federation rescue team led by Lieutenant El‑An just as it passes through the Portal. Sola, terrified but resilient, is brought to Errahel, the Pleiadian homeworld, a place of crystalline spires, gentle succulents, and a constant scent of gardenia. She is given lodging, a Sil’on uniform, and the nourishing gel N’aaj. In her quarters, she finds a framed text titled “The Twelve Laws of the Universe,” adapted from Pleiadian spirituality. The laws—ranging from the Law of Consciousness and the Law of One to the Law of Free Will and the Law of Manifestation—codify the book’s spiritual framework: that all is one consciousness, that love is the highest vibration, that reality responds to thought and emotion, and that every being is a sovereign creator co‑creating with the universe.

Sola is also taken before the Council of Light, where Lord Levian formally asks her to accept her role as a “gridkeeper.” Panicked by the weight of expectation, Sola initially refuses and runs out. El‑An then guides her through a profound inner experience using a large selenite crystal. In a meditative state, Sola revisits traumatic memories of her mother’s overdose and her own childhood, seeing angels present throughout. She forgives her mother, releases guilt, and understands that none of her mother’s struggles are her fault. This emotional breakthrough opens her heart, and she realizes she must protect her family and home. When she sees a holographic projection of a Draconian‑generated superstom threatening to destroy Key West and her father’s boat, she returns to Lord Levian and accepts the mission.

**The Ley Line and the Bermuda Triangle**

Sola is returned to Earth via the Portal, rejoining Blu, Astria, and the now‑healed Cap. Vera and Atlas brief them: Sola must sail to the Bermuda Triangle, which is actually an interdimensional portal left from Atlantis, and deposit a crystalline key—an Ankh—at a specific ley‑line coordinate to anchor the chaotic grid node and avert catastrophe. The mission must be completed by daybreak.

The boat journey is harrowing. A Draconian ship intercepts them, and two Nagaai board. During the struggle, a waterspout tears the enemy craft apart. Sola is knocked overboard but is rescued by two dolphins—Jade and Jason—who bring her back to the boat. A giant whale named Bo later appears, shielding them from the worst of the storm. The super‑squall intensifies as the box containing the Ankh is washed overboard. Sola dives into the deep, following her clairsentient pull to the ley line’s epicenter. Using the Ankh, she thrusts it into the vortex and, in her mind, recites the activation phrase. A shockwave of green light stabilizes the grid, the storm instantly dissipates, and the sky clears. Blu pulls Sola, unconscious, from the water and revives her.

**Revelations and Parallel Selves**

Back on shore, Vera reveals a deeper truth to Sola: Vera is a parallel version of Sola, existing at a different point in the same space/time continuum. Both are facets of the same oversoul, working together across dimensions to facilitate Disclosure Day. Vera’s constant sunglasses are explained: if their eyes were to meet, the timeline would collapse. Sola, though stunned, accepts the connection. Vera and Atlas then return to Errahel, promising to stay in touch through meditation. Before leaving, Vera gives Sola a silver locket with Metatron’s Cube on the back and a hidden compartment for urgent contact.

**Earthly Threads and Growing Threats**

While the galactic drama unfolds, several human‑scale storylines deepen. The bullies Drex and Led steal the Pleiadian Sil’on suits from Astria’s trailer, hoping to exploit their protective powers. Drex, who is revealed to be a foster child with a violent home life, has a strange encounter with an old man who calls himself Korvus. Korvus, in his Draconian identity, has tracked his own energetic “fractal”—Drex—and slips him a glowing crypto‑key, planting a seed of dark influence. The key later helps Drex locate the hidden suits, but the theft is thwarted by Atlas and Vera.

Sola’s romantic horizon expands with the arrival of James Foley, Cap’s British great‑nephew, who moves to Key West with his younger sister, Louise. Sola is smitten. During a trip to a sandbar with James, Blu, and Louise, Sola notices a mysterious black boat in the distance. Later, after a heart‑pounding walk home through a thunderstorm, Sola and James share their first kiss under glowing braids and lightning.

But the moment is undercut by foreboding. Sola receives an anonymous text with a photo: it shows Katie Lane, the school mean girl, petting a large iguana with glowing amber eyes, while Louise sits zombie‑like in the background. The message warns, “Be careful who you trust.” Sola realizes that the Draconian threat is far from over, and that the darker forces have already insinuated themselves into her social circle.

Simultaneously, the United States government—alerted by the massive energy spike from the ley‑line activation—has dispatched agents to monitor Sola. They are now in a black speedboat, listening with directional microphones. The novel ends on this chilling note: Sola’s true test as a gridkeeper is only beginning.

**The Broader Tapestry**

Throughout the story, the author weaves in extensive metaphysical lore. The Galactic Federation of Worlds, led by Kai El, is depicted as a benevolent but firm overseer, guided by principles of free will and service to others. The Orion‑Draconian Empire, ruled by Nyxar, represents service‑to‑self polarity, using fear and mind control. The Twelce Laws serve as the novel’s philosophical backbone, explaining how consciousness, vibration, and free will shape reality.

The concept of hybrid children is central: Yahyel, half‑human, half‑Grey beings, are being prepared as ambassadors for first contact. Sola’s childhood “imaginary friends” turn out to be such hybrids, including a girl named Aysha. In a late twist, it is revealed that the U.S. military has begun abducting some of these hybrid children for study, setting up the need for Vera and Atlas’s next mission.

Key characters’ relationships evolve under this pressure. Blu, initially terrified of the unknown, grows into a brave and loyal friend, using his Sil’on suit to save Drex from a crocodile attack and later helping to bring Led toward redemption. Astria and Cap rekindle a sweet romance, while Astria’s quiet spiritual authority grounds the entire team. Sola’s healing of her relationship with her mother, Alana, becomes the emotional hinge that allows her to accept her cosmic duty.

The book ends with a clear trajectory: Sola must eventually anchor the remaining two ley lines, the hybrid children must be rescued, and Disclosure Day looms. Katie, Drex, Korvus, and the government agents are all positioned as immediate threats, while James’s genuine affection and Blu’s steadfast support give Sola a human foundation. The final line reaffirms the novel’s central invitation: everything that happened was not just about Sola; it was also, through the act of reading, about raising the vibration of the reader and the collective, moving Earth closer to its own Disclosure Day.
