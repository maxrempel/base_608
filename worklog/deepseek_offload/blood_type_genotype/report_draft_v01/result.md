# PRELIMINARY Report: ABO and RhD Enrichment in Self-Reported Starseed Participants
**Date:** 2026-07-27

## Question, Answer, and Uncertainty

**Question:** Are O blood group or RhD-negative status unusually common among the available genotyped self-reported Starseed participants?

**Answer:** Current data do not support O-negative enrichment. Among five designated participants, the ABO distribution shows three A, one O, zero B, and zero AB. RhD proxy data exist for only two of five participants; both proxies favor RhD-positive. No testable parent-child transmission inconsistency was observed. The sample is too small and RhD coverage too incomplete to estimate prevalence or rule out an effect.

**Uncertainty:** High. Only 5 designated participants with confirmed identity; 2 of 5 lacked RhD proxy data entirely; one ABO call was low-confidence due to discordant O markers. The total genotyped cohort is 28 consumer-genotype exports representing 25 unique people across 7 families, but only the 5 designated participants were included in the hypothesis count. Two other families had no proven designated-participant identity and were excluded per study design.

## Methods

### Study Design
We analyzed deidentified consumer-genotype exports from self-reported Starseed participants. Only participants whose designated identity could be documented were included in the hypothesis test (n=5). Participants from families without proven designated identity were excluded.

### ABO Genotyping
ABO blood group was inferred using a four-tag single-nucleotide polymorphism panel (rs507666 A=A1, rs8176704 A=A2, rs8176746 T=B, rs687289 G=O) with rs505922 T as a fallback O proxy (primary reference: PMCID PMC7669452). The four tag counts must reconcile to exactly two alleles per participant (e.g., AO, OO, AA, etc.). The common O1 deletion marker rs8176719 and O2 marker rs41302905 were used as consistency checks; conflicts were downgraded in confidence. Missing markers were left unresolved (never imputed as positive or negative). Algorithm limitations due to ancestry-dependent tag performance have been described (reference: PMCID PMC11631141).

### RhD Genotyping
RhD status was approximated using rs590787 as an ancestry-dependent proxy (reference: PMCID PMC9012157). Consumer arrays do not directly characterize the common whole-RHD deletion, copy number, weak D, partial D, pseudogene, or hybrid alleles. Therefore, absence of RhD proxy data does not imply RhD-negative; it is recorded as unresolved.

### Extended Blood Groups
Where the relevant causal marker was directly present, research predictions were generated for: Kell, Kidd, Duffy, MNS S/s, RHCE E/e, Diego, Lutheran, and FUT2 secretor status (references: PMCID PMC7036580 and PMCID PMC7263432). These remain private participant-level outputs and were not used in the main hypothesis.

### Parent-Child Transmission Consistency
ABO genotypes were assessed for Mendelian consistency across the seven families. No inconsistency was observed.

## Validation

- **Independent replicate:** One family was typed independently by both 23andMe and MyHeritage; both services agreed on shared interpreted systems.
- **Tag panel reliability:** The four-tag ABO panel has been validated in multiple ancestry groups (PMCID PMC7669452). Discordance flags (e.g., O1 deletion marker mismatching the O tag) were applied, resulting in one low-confidence A call.
- **No data fabrication:** All reported results derive directly from raw genotyping exports. Missing markers are explicitly noted.

## Results

- **Cohort:** 28 consumer-genotype exports, 25 unique individuals, 7 families.
- **Designated participants with confirmed identity:** 5.
- **ABO resolved (4/5):** A=3, O=1, B=0, AB=0. One participant was unresolved.
- **Confidence in ABO calls:** 1 high-confidence (research call based on full tag reconciliation + O1 deletion concordance), 2 moderate (tag-panel calls with no discordance but lacking full deletion marker data), 1 low-confidence (O tag disagreed with common O1 deletion marker; final call as A with caution).
- **RhD proxy** present in only 2/5 designated participants: both proxies favor RhD-positive. The remaining three are unresolved (no reliable proxy data).
- **Extended predictions** (Kell, Kidd, etc.) were generated where causal markers existed but remain private participant-level outputs.

## Safe Conclusion

Current data do not support O-negative enrichment among this small sample. The observed ABO distribution (3 A, 1 O) is consistent with general population frequencies (roughly 40–45% A and 40–45% O in many ancestral groups), though formal statistical testing is inappropriate given n=4 resolved ABO calls. RhD coverage is insufficient even for a prevalence estimate.

**These are research predictions only, never clinical or transfusion-safe.** No participant should consider these results for medical decisions.

## Limitations

1. **Sample size:** Very small (n=5 for hypothesis count; n=4 for ABO). Any apparent enrichment or lack thereof could arise from chance.
2. **Incomplete RhD characterization:** The proxy SNP (rs590787) is ancestry-dependent and does not detect the common RHD deletion, weak D, partial D, or hybrid alleles. Thus, RhD-negative status cannot be ruled out in the three unresolved participants.
3. **Low-confidence ABO call:** One A call is uncertain due to discordance between tag-based O inference and the direct O1 deletion marker.
4. **Consumer genotyping limitations:** Arrays are designed for ancestry and health traits, not clinical blood typing. Allele dropout, copy-number variation, and ancestry-specific tag failure are possible.
5. **No independent serology:** No blood sample, no direct agglutination test, no transfusion-history data.
6. **Excluded families:** Two families without proven designated-participant identity were excluded; they could contain additional data but could not be used for this hypothesis test.
7. **No population enrichment estimate:** The sample is too small and RhD coverage too incomplete to calculate any odds ratio or enrichment statistic.

## Immediate Next Step

Obtain high-resolution sequencing or copy-number analysis for RHD in all five designated participants to resolve RhD status definitively. Expand the cohort by recruiting additional self-reported Starseed participants with documented identity and direct-to-consumer or clinical genotyping data. Consider collecting either a serological blood type (direct agglutination) or a validated clinical-grade genotyping panel (e.g., for all major blood group systems) to enable robust prevalence estimation.
