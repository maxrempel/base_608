Question: Are O blood group or RhD-negative status unusually common among the
available genotyped self-reported Starseed participants?

Data and results:

- 28 consumer-genotype exports, 25 unique people, 7 families.
- Five documented designated participants; two other families have no proven
  designated-participant identity and were excluded from the hypothesis count.
- ABO resolved in 4/5 designated participants: A=3, O=1, B=0, AB=0.
- Confidence: one high-confidence research call, two moderate tag-panel calls,
  and one low-confidence A call because an O tag disagreed with the common O1
  deletion marker. One participant was unresolved.
- RhD proxy was present in only 2/5 designated participants; both proxies favor
  RhD-positive. The other three are unresolved.
- No testable ABO parent-child transmission inconsistency was observed.
- One family typed independently by 23andMe and MyHeritage agreed on shared
  interpreted systems.
- Extended research predictions were generated where the relevant causal
  marker was directly present: Kell, Kidd, Duffy, MNS S/s, RHCE E/e, Diego,
  Lutheran, and FUT2 secretor status. These remain private participant-level
  outputs.
- Safe conclusion: current data do not support O-negative enrichment. The
  sample is too small and RhD coverage too incomplete to estimate prevalence
  or rule out an effect.
- These are research predictions only, never clinical or transfusion-safe.

Methods:

- ABO four-tag inference uses rs507666 A=A1, rs8176704 A=A2,
  rs8176746 T=B, rs687289 G=O, with rs505922 T only as a fallback O proxy.
  The four counts must reconcile to two alleles. Common O1 deletion rs8176719
  and O2 rs41302905 are consistency checks; conflicts are downgraded.
- RhD uses rs590787 only as an ancestry-dependent proxy. Consumer arrays do
  not directly characterize the common whole-RHD deletion, copy number, weak D,
  partial D, pseudogene, or hybrid alleles.
- Missing markers are unresolved, never imputed as positive or negative.
