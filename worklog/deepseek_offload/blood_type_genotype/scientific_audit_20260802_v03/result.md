### TLDR
This analysis cannot determine ABO/Rh blood type for any self-reported Starseed focal participant, and no evidence of an unusual O/Rh-negative enrichment was found. Broad ABO calls were 3 predicted A, 1 predicted O, 1 unresolved; strict calls were 2 A, 1 O, 1 excluded, 1 unresolved. A-vs-non-A Fisher tests were nonsignificant (broad p = .0885; strict p = .2237) with enormous confidence intervals. RhD was untestable.

### Safe interpretation
The results are inconclusive and should not be used as blood-type evidence. The observed ABO distribution is compatible with chance given the small, ancestry-unmatched focal sample and ancestry-dependent control attrition. No clinical phenotype records exist, so even a nominally significant genotype call would not validate a blood-type claim. The Rh-negative/O-negative folklore was not tested because the arrays cannot assess RHD deletion, copy number, weak/partial D, pseudogene, or RHD/RHCE hybrids; rs590787 is only an ancestry-dependent proxy.

### Main limitations
- Only five focal participants, self-selected and self-reported; no matched ancestry/platform controls.
- Classifier is unphased and based on four tags; cannot exclude rare ABO alleles, cis/trans ambiguity, recombinants, weak A/B, unusual O, Bombay/para-Bombay, or structural alleles.
- Control attrition is ancestry-dependent (AFR 109/339 unresolved vs EUR 13/493, SAS 0/103), biasing all comparisons; focal ancestry/platform are unmatched.
- No multiple-testing correction across 24 exploratory contrasts.
- Family/platform checks passed but do not validate phenotype; same-classifier controls have unknown true ABO phenotypes.
- RhD uncallable for all five; all Rh-related conclusions are unsupported.
- Missing genotypes were never treated as reference, but remaining uncertainty is still high.

### Smallest zero-cost next step
Do not buy new genomes. Calibrate the four-tag classifier against phased 1000 Genomes ABO-region sequence, stratified by superpopulation, to produce confusion matrices and probabilistic confidence for each observed tag pattern. Then separately solicit any existing documented ABO/Rh records from focal participants, and ask whether knowledge of or folklore about “Starseed” blood types predated their self-identification. That would distinguish genotype-classifier error from recall/selection bias and would give the first meaningful prior for interpreting any future focal calls.
