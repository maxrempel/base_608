Part One: Opening Context and the First Questions

The channeler opened by apologizing that his computer was giving him trouble, joking about Mercury retrograde, and offered Jen either a chat first or an immediate channeling. Jen said she had loved following his Japan travels, and he reported that his children were doing well and that he had enough support while traveling; life was busy but not badly chaotic. Jen explained that she had booked the session on impulse, and that she had gone back to an old recording in which she asked how to help humanity’s ascension and Ardif said she could offer her knowledge through fiction writing. At the time she ignored it, but three years later she had written a young adult trilogy, The Disclosure Day Chronicles, channeling at least two hundred pages and writing about the Pleiadians. She promised to send him a gold-foiled hardcover with the flower of the universe on the front. She noted synchronicities: she published on June 2, Pentagon soft-disclosure announcements came days later, and Spielberg’s Disclosure Day movie opened June 12. The channeler added that extraterrestrials were announced real on the same day as a channel panel named “Contact Event.”

Jen said she had put together random questions and asked whether he pulls in whoever comes forward. He offered Treb, Ardif, or any excited being. She asked for whoever was excited, and he suggested starting with Treb this time because Treb tends to invite other beings through, whereas Ardif almost always comes alone. He credited Wendy Kennedy with helping him learn to enter trance in about a minute rather than ten. Treb then greeted Jen, saying he was excited to cocreate and was ready for her questions.

Her first question was about past-life or between-life agreements shaping her closest relationships. Treb said free will rides in front of the entire decision system, and the higher self is like the person holding a laser pointer for a kitten: it shows where to run, but following any pre-incarnation contract is optional. He described linear time as merely a perspective, with past versions just as probable as futures. He then described a connected past life in early 1600s Egypt, where she was a trader who found a large dark purple crystal wrapped in copper. Local priests meditated with it and taught her their meditation, bringing months of dream states in which she connected with beings she perceived as gods. She eventually lost interest in trading, traveled the African coast, and by age forty-five had taught meditation to many tribes.

Asked about her most active galactic or star-family connection, Treb named Pleiadian, Sirian, and Arcturian as the three most prominent. Asked what she needed to release before the October Venus retrograde recalibration, he said humans generally must break down walls around receiving love and feelings of worthiness, and that she should integrate more masculine energy to balance her first and second chakras and, by proxy, the solar plexus, while continuing to trust intuition without judgment.

For her primary spirit guides, Treb said these differ from human perceptions and have no fixed identity because they have been with her across many incarnations. Four guides were strongest now: two humanoid fifth-density Pleiadians from near Electra representing feminine and masculine duality; a tall, slender, blue, hairless humanoid from Octores; and a Sirian aquatic fifth-density being with a pragmatic mix of logic and emotion. They work mainly in the background with telepathy, intuition, and psychic ability. Jen thanked them.

She asked whether F. Scott Fitzgerald was part of her soul family circle; Treb said yes, as an over-soul connection, and Jen said she felt he had helped her writing. Asked about a deeper thread connecting her lifetimes with Taylor Wells and Laura Popink, Treb said both are in her greater over-soul family and have played different roles across lives, in this lifetime helping her learn to give and receive energy and balance heart-chakra self-perception, though he saw no single specific dynamic.

Jen recalled that Ardif had once told her to write fiction, and asked for the highest-impact path for The Disclosure Day Chronicles. Treb advised continuing to share her excitement and integrating the story into media outlets or willing channels, even on her own, through small narrations, illustrations, or a short animated portion, so audiences meet the characters in a whimsical way. He suggested finding people through social media with whom she can simply share narrations.
