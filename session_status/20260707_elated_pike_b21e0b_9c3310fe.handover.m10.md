## GOTCHAS

1. **"Maps to human" ? dismiss.** This was the session's most persistent and costly error. The target IS human-like DNA (~0.5-10% divergence from consensus, out-of-place). Dismissing something as "known human non-reference insertion" throws away the actual signal. The correct filter is: maps to *same* locus = ordinary; maps to *distant* locus at 90-95% identity + rare = candidate.

2. **"Absent in mother" without phasing = ambiguous.** A child inherits ~half his father's variants, all "absent in mother." Only phasing (proving the insert sits on the maternal copy specifically) discriminates de-novo from paternal. This is the hard data limit on short reads: ~131 large candidates and hundreds more in the smaller bands are mother-absent but UNPHASEABLE because no informative marker is within read-reach.

3. **Divergence measured against GRCh38 alone is misleading.** A sequence at 91% identity to GRCh38 that maps at 100% to T2T-CHM13 or a human fosmid clone is NOT diverged - it's just missing from the old reference. Always check against T2T + nt-BLAST before calling a divergence.

4. **Repeat-window trap (the mother-false-absent bug).** A payload from near a repeat (centromere, segdup, acrocentric) looks "absent" in the mother because the narrow read-window doesn't capture her duplicated copy elsewhere. The fix: always check mother-absence in a wider window by requiring the mother to have *coverage* at the site but zero reads carrying the insert. The funnel table confirms: 0 "can't-tell" (all had coverage). But when I look at individual loci, the trap recurred - 6 of the 16 residual candidates turned out to be IN the mother when checked at the actual breakpoint.

5. **Internet "dead" was an assumption, not a measurement.** Max: "you must run the speed test, always." FIXT: asto actually had ~157 Mbps. The nt-BLAST and T2T downloads were unblocked. Lesson: never defer on an untested assumption about resources.

6. **ChrY is trivially mother-absent** (mother is XX). The main funnel had 51-59 chrY inserts in the "absent" pile - ordinary paternal Y, not a signal. Always strip chrY before reporting.

7. **Positive control passes but is minimal:** a synthetic 100 bp insert spiked into chr20 was recovered correctly. The detector finds insertions; the issue is distinguishing ordinary from candidate, not detection failure.

8. **The 51-150 bp seam genuinely needed a new detector.** BWA does not write a CIGAR insertion above ~50 bp (confirmed: zero read-level inserts >50 bp on chr20) - it soft-clips instead. And it's too small for the large-insert junction rebuild. The working method: local-assemble reads at two-sided soft-clip clusters, then read the assembled contig's alignment for a 51-150 bp internal insertion.

9. **Kristen's count (349 large) vs Oliver's (743) is a live warning.** Unknown if this is biology, coverage difference, or an alignment artifact (both are bwa, so it's likely biology - but Kristen was never run as her own index through controls, so her "normal" status is assumed, not measured).
