## GOAL (Max's own words, verbatim from final reframe)

Aliens are distant human relatives. ## DECISIONS + WHY

1. **Option B (fish-to-EXTEND, not close):** Detect insertions via two half-chimera contigs - human-anchored on one end, foreign overhang on the other - meeting at one locus. Chosen because it's length-independent (a 1 kb and 50 kb insert read the same; we never need to close the bridge). Validated: positive control `make_pc.sh` passes at 1 kb and 5 kb.

2. **Abandoned Sol for genomics:** Sol's hardware corrupts the 35 GB BAM on write (two independent copies gave two random CRC32/BGZF offsets). Team rule: no genomics on Sol. Kristen's BAM was re-transferred with md5 verification.

3. **EC2 was abandoned** when real upload speed measured: asto?us-west-2 = ~2.7 MB/s (~3.6h for 35 GB). X12B's EC2 works because 1000-Genomes data is public and in-region (instant read); our private BAM is behind a slow uplink, so compute stays where the data lives.

4. **Oliver's genome used, not Kristen's vendor BAM:** Kristen's DRAGEN vendor BAM has ~8? fewer soft-clips than bwa (chr21: 27.5k vs 228k at ?30 bp), confirmed by X5. Oliver's fresh bwa realign is the good data for OMEGA. Kristen's bwa realign was produced overnight by X5 for the mother-son comparison.

5. **Phasing replaces the father requirement:** X8A produced phased haplotypes for Oliver and Kristen, with a per-block maternal-side table. This lets us check "is the insert on the maternal haplotype AND absent from the mother?" - de-novo on the maternal copy - without needing the father's genome.

6. **Mate-pair extension for phasing reach:** When no phased het is within a single read (~150 bp) of the insert, the mate pair (~500 bp farther) extends reach into phased phase blocks. Implemented in `phase_matelink.py`. Two strongest "mother-absent" candidates (chr3, chr6) were both proven **paternal** - the insert rides the paternal allele.

7. **Relocated-diverged target class was previously mis-discarded:** The earlier pass filtered paralog-mapping pieces as "artifacts." Max's reframe fixed this: those are exactly the target. A cleanly-resolved relocated copy with consistent diagnostic mutations across uniquely-mapping reads is a candidate. The discriminator is **consistent vs mixed** alleles - a real copy shows the same mutations on every read; a mismap smear shows inconsistent alleles.

8. **Folder tidied into reports/ scripts/ data/small/ data/large/:** reports are durable markdown in git, scripts are the code, data/small holds the small valuable products (payloads, tables), data/large is a README pointing to heavy regenerable files on asto. Survives asto being wiped.

4. **150 bp length floor was arbitrary.** Max explicitly questioned it; 30-50 bp inserts should be counted. Pilot done - mostly microsatellites, but the question is open.

5. **Phasing is MANDATORY for any de-novo claim.** "Absent in mother" alone doesn't count - the insert could be on the paternal chromosome. Must confirm maternal-haplotype de-novo. The two strongest "mother-absent" candidates (chr3, chr6) were both proven paternal once phased.

6. **K-mers vs direct comparison:** Max questioned why k-mers are used. The reality: an insertion isn't a reference "position" - the inserted letters live only inside the reads themselves, not in the reference. So you can't read a base off the reference at that spot. K-mers are asking "does the mother's read contain the inserted sequence" - aligner-agnostic. But for small inserts (<50 bp) they're actually visible as "insertion marks" inside the read alignment - those can be directly compared without k-mers. The closer translation: the direct test is going to the locus in the mother's reads and asking "do any reads dangle off into the same inserted piece" (the clipping signature). That's the mirror of how they were found in the son.
