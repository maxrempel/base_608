## GOAL (Max's own words)

Max bought Tile hardware on Amazon. It connects via Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. The phone-finding-keys direction works great - from Android, he can make the keys beep. But the reverse direction (keys-to-phone) always fails: the Tile app on Android goes inactive and never wakes up again. After reactivation, it works for a day or two, then falls asleep again.

He wants **an Android application that does not die, is resumable, and will wake up Tile as soon as it gets asleep - or periodically - so the double-press on the key tag reliably wakes the phone.**

---

## DECISIONS + WHY

### Decision 1: Two distinct technical problems exist, not one.
The request actually splits into (A) keeping a listener alive forever, and (B) actually detecting the Tile button press at the protocol level.

- **(A) Keeping a listener alive** - straightforward. A foreground service with a persistent notification is the standard Android mechanism for an undying, resumable process. This is buildable.

- **(B) Detecting the double-press yourself** - hard and uncertain. The button-to-phone signal uses Tile's proprietary protocol, which is often encrypted on newer devices. A third-party app cannot guarantee it can "hear" the key press without knowing what to listen for.

### Decision 2: Feasibility hinges entirely on the Tile model.
Claude assessed that the whole project's viability depends on which Tile hardware Max owns (model and year), because older Tiles may expose the signal more plainly, while newer ones almost certainly don't.

### Decision 3: A free OS-level fix exists as a lower-effort alternative.
Disabling battery optimization for the Tile app and locking it in the recent apps list often solves the Doze/kill problem entirely. Claude offered this as a first step before any custom app build.

## GOTCHAS / DEAD ENDS RULED OUT

- **Don't assume a third-party app can listen for Tile button presses.** The Tile protocol is proprietary. Reverse-engineering it is non-trivial and may be outright impossible on newer hardware with encrypted BLE advertisements. If Max has a recent Tile model, the custom-app approach may need to pivot to: (a) periodically waking/re-launching the official Tile app via intents or alarms, rather than (b) replacing Tile's BLE listener entirely. This distinction was flagged early to avoid building something that cannot work.

- **Android Doze / aggressive battery management is the likely root cause** of the Tile app dying. A foreground service solves the "my app stays alive" part, but if the real fix is "keep the official Tile app alive," the strategy shifts toward a keep-alive helper rather than a replacement listener.
