## GOAL (Max's words)

Auto-index timecodes for concert videos on tamza.com. Use DeepSeek ("ds4flash") to read transcripts, search online for song names/authors, flag self-authored songs, merge duplicates. The core insight: build a **fuzzy full-text index** of all songs in the catalog - even poor transcription quality will match a full lyric block, massively increasing identification. Human timecoders handle the problematic cases via a game-like interface (face-matching for performer identification, bingo sounds).

The immediate target: index the **~45% of videos currently missing** (1049 unindexed out of 1558 total), learning from the existing human-curated catalog.

---

## STRATEGY (decided + why)

**Precision-first, no guessing.** The machine does two things:
1. **Time-code every song boundary** (start/end), even for unnamed songs.
2. **Put a name on a segment ONLY when certain** - either a full-text lyric match or a clearly spoken announcement of authorship.

Everything not confidently auto-labeled stays **blank** and goes to the human team's game UI. An honest blank beats a wrong label. Max's term: **"failed to identify"** (not "wrong") for blanks.

**Source of confidence for labeling:**
- **Recurrence** - 50-70% of songs recur across concerts. Proven: ~53% of songs in a leave-one-video-out test already exist in the other videos' human-curated catalog.
- **Full-text matching** - matching the transcript against full lyrics (not just song titles) survives bad transcription. This is the biggest lever to lift the machine's share above the measured ~36% floor.
- **Announce detection** - DeepSeek reads each transcript for spoken announcements. Unannounced + not-in-corpus = flag as likely self-authored.
