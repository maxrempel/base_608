# HANDOVER: B14 Indexer - Kartoteka Song Auto-Indexing

## GOAL (in Max's own words)
*"Identify only what is clearly coming out - either a match by full text or a clearly announced authorship. And time coding everything. Then the human team will check and fill the blanks."*

The broader vision: auto-index timecodes for concert videos by matching transcripts against the ~26k-song human-curated catalog. Use fuzzy full-text matching to massively boost identification rates (poor transcription quality still matches full lyric blocks). For unmatched songs, DeepSeek ("ds") reads the transcript for performer/author announcements; unannounced songs get flagged as likely self-authored. Humans get a gamified interface to match faces/names for the hard cases.

The immediate pragmatic constraint: **only ~55% of videos are currently human-indexed.** The job is to learn from the existing curated catalog and then index the missing ~45% from scratch.

---

## DECISIONS MADE + WHY

1. **Approach: learn from curated catalog, then index from scratch (Option A, not B)**
   - B was "just enrich existing catalog metadata." Rejected. The real bottleneck is the unindexed ~45% of videos.

2. **Validation prototype: leave-one-video-out testing**
   - Built `idx_validate_v01.py` that tests whether a video's songs can be identified using ONLY the *other* videos' curated titles. Zero cost, zero YouTube harvest, fully offline.
   - Ran on 25 videos / 2172 songs. Results proved the concept works.

3. **Two complementary signals identified:**
   - **Recurrence (47%):** exact-normalized title matches against the catalog of known songs from OTHER videos. Confirms Max's estimate that 50-70% of songs repeat across concerts.
   - **Transcript-location (63%):** the song title literally appears in the transcript (either announced by the performer OR sung as a refrain). 68% of these land within 60 seconds of the human's ground-truth start time.
   - These signals are additive - combined recall exceeds either alone.

4. **Three-step architecture outlined (not yet built):**
   - Step 1: Collect all texts (transcripts). 452 already harvested. Scaling needs coordination with b9 who is using the home IP for a 1,558-video backup - mass transcript fetching would collide.
   - Step 2: Pull lyrics/texts from the internet for songs NOT already in the catalog. This is the biggest lever - full lyrics matching survives bad transcription.
   - Step 3: Merge/dedup, apply announce-heuristic via DeepSeek, flag unannounced songs as likely self-authored.

5. **Max's refinement on the final approach (most recent):**
   - Narrow the scope: ONLY identify clear matches (full-text match OR clearly announced authorship). Don't try to guess the ambiguous ones.
   - Timecode everything that IS identified.
   - Leave the rest as blanks for the human team.

6. **Terminology resolved:** "ds4flash" = DeepSeek ("ds") used as the cheap/fast LLM to read transcripts ("flash"). Not a separate tool name - a method.
