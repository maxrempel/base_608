## DECISIONS + WHY

### The merged-lipsie method
- **Why merges:** Instead of one lipsie per line (old method), merge 2-7 consecutive lines into ONE lipsie using a character two-shot still. The merged audio carries all lines, the prompt labels who speaks when.
- **Why ~4 lines optimal:** 15-second clip cap on wan2.6. Most lines are short, so 2-7 fit. Two monologues (line 8 = 14s, line 23 = 13s) are forced singles - they nearly fill a clip alone.
- **Why different stills per location:** The scene walks a path: greeting hall ? corridor walk ? window with Earth ? alcove ? doorway/room. Each beat needs its location's two-shot still.

### Prompt recipe (hard-won through many failures)
- **NO smile/grin words** - wan2.6 turns them into random laughter or excessive smiling. Removed entirely.
- **Describe both characters first** - names + positions stated BEFORE the dialogue lines. Fixes the speaker-swap problem where wan ignores bare "Left/Right" labels.
- **Speak-order phrasing** - "the red-haired woman speaks first, then the elder answers." The only thing that reliably fixes speaker assignment in dialogues.
- **Listener stillness** - "only the speaker's lips move; the listener stays still" works for standing shots, but FREEZES hands in walking shots (hand frozen mid-air looks robotic).
- **Walking shots: neutral hands** - don't direct hands at all. "Still" froze them, "arms moving naturally" made them robotic. Best result came from not mentioning hands.
- **In profile, eyes on each other** - "keep looking at each other" prevents the camera-gaze problem (looking at a third party).
- **Composed throughout** - instruction to keep expression composed before/during/after the line, preventing the smile-at-the-end problem.
