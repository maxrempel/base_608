## DECISIONS MADE + WHY

### Diagnosis (completed, four independent confirmations)
1. **Not heat, not PSU, not CPU, not NIC.** Sequential isolation: CPU-only load survived 5 minutes full tilt; memory-only load crashed Sol in ~2.5 minutes.
2. **Bad RAM (failing DIMM).** Captured a kernel panic with a single-bit-flipped pointer (`0xfeff...` where it should be `0xffff...` - exactly one bit stuck high). Non-ECC RAM can't self-correct.
3. **Fault is HEAT/LOAD-triggered.** Static cold-boot tests pass clean every time. The bit-flip only manifests once the DIMM warms under sustained memory stress.
4. **4 independent confirmations:** (a) the kernel panic pointer flip, (b) stress-ng VM crash, (c) memtester caught it red-handed with bit-pattern failures, (d) custom ramscan.c crash.

### Software-block attempt (FAILED - dead end)
Max wanted us to map bad cells' physical addresses and reserve them in the bootloader (`memmap=` in GRUB) so Linux never uses them - the "software bad-sector remap." I built a custom C scanner (ramscan.c), a reboot-survivable mapping loop, and ran it. **The bad cells are NOT a fixed set.** They move every scan (113 bad pages in run1, then 22 in run2, then 220 in run3, all different scattered addresses, all single-bit flips). That means it's a whole marginal stick, not a few dead locations. **You can't software-block a moving target.** Max was ultimately right: "what's the point of mapping."

### Intel channel-interleaving
The 4 sticks are smeared across the physical address space. The OS is never told which address belongs to which slot. So "disable a whole stick in software" is architecturally impossible - only a physical pull or BIOS slot-disable removes a whole stick.

### The crash-loop was too aggressive (Sol froze)
My unattended auto-crash-loop (reboot-survivable ramscan service) was too aggressive and froze Sol solid - needed Max to physically force-start it. The crash-loop (`sol-ramscan-loop.service`) is now **disabled** and must stay that way. Lesson: one attended scan per boot, no self-rebooting crash loops.

### Identification math (settled with Max)
How many boots to positively identify exactly which 1 of 4 sticks is bad, while also clearing the 3 good ones (positive + negative ID): **3 boots** via group testing.
- Boot 1: pair {A,B}. Crash ? bad is in this pair, and the OTHER pair {C,D} is automatically neg-cleared.
- Boots 2&3: A solo, B solo. Whichever crashes = positively bad; the other = positively good.
- Max's scheme, independently confirmed by him - I over-complicated it twice through two wrong answers before agreeing with his original 3.
