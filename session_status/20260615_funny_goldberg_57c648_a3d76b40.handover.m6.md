The Kidde alarm is 2.4 GHz-only, so it saw neighbors' networks but nothing from this house. The "Rempel" Max saw on his phone was the 5 GHz network - phones do 5 GHz; the alarm doesn't.

2. **Temporary "KiddeSetup" created, then deleted.** Initially stood up a separate 2.4 GHz SSID to get the alarm paired quickly without touching main networks. After Max objected to "proliferation of networks" / "EMF pollution," it was removed. This was a clean surgical procedure - no leftover config.

3. **"Rempel" moved to both bands.** Max's instruction: "delete ks, move rempel to 2.4g." Result: one SSID ("Rempel") broadcasts on both 5 GHz (channel 149, for fast devices) and 2.4 GHz (channel 11, 20 MHz width, for the alarm and other IoT). Same password on both: `razdvatri`.

4. **"maxhome" is NOT Max's network.** It's a neighbor or other nearby device. Confirmed by checking router config - not present.

5. **Physics/EMF discussion happened.** Max expressed concern about "EMF pollution" from multiple networks. Clarified that extra SSID *names* on the same radio add negligible RF (shared transmitter, shared channel - just a few extra beacon frames). The real EMF change was turning the 2.4 GHz radio back on, which is unavoidable for a 2.4 GHz-only smart alarm. Also discussed 2.4 vs 5 GHz health differences - both non-ionizing, both below harm thresholds; practical lever is distance from router, not band choice.
