## DECISIONS + WHY

1. **Root cause of all previous failures**  
   The entire Wan 2.2 S2V model stack (diffusion model, text encoder, audio encoder, VAE) was symlinked onto `green24`, a 24 TB exFAT spinning drive. Every sampler step re-read ~16 GB at ~28-36 MB/s, leaving the GPU at 0% utilisation and the disk 85 % busy. This made renders unbearably slow and forced too few steps, so faces melted and identity drifted.  
   **Fix:** copy all four model files to the NVMe (native ext4/home, /dev/nvme0n1). After the move, weight streaming went to PCIe speed and render time dropped from ~7+ minutes per step to a steady 48 s/step.

2. **Framing and clip length**  
   The old tests used a wide shot where the character's head was ~100 px tall. The mouth lacked enough pixels to resolve lip shapes, so it smeared. Long (10?second) multi-chunk runs also drifted identity across chunks.  
   **Fix:** crop input to a medium-closeup (face fills ~1/3 of frame) and limit to a single 5?second chunk matching the audio duration. No drift.

3. **Sampler and settings**  
   The official Alibaba/Comfy template for Wan 2.2 S2V calls for `uni_pc` sampler with shift=8, cfg=6, 20 steps, sage attention enabled. Earlier runs used `euler` and possibly wrong shift, contributing to instability.  
   **Fix:** use `uni_pc`, shift 8, cfg 6, 20 steps, with sage attention installed and active.

4. **Model choice**  
   MoMA's documented preferred lip-sync model is `wan2.2_s2v_14B_fp8_scaled`. That was already on disk but mis?located. No need to download a different model. Attempted GGUF quant to save VRAM, but even Q5_K_M was 15 GB - no advantage over fp8 when models live on fast storage. Dropped that path.

5. **VRAM management**  
   Taygeta's Whisper dictation server (`typer_stt` on port 8123) permanently holds ~3.9 GB of VRAM. Max explicitly wanted it left alone. The Wan 2.2 model with fp8 precision stages at ~15.6 GB, fitting inside the 16 GB RTX 5060 Ti alongside Whisper - with no OOM, albeit at the card's limit. This is why the step time is 48 s (some offloading and small headroom).

6. **Validation method**  
   To prove lipsync is genuinely audio?driven, ran a control: identical seed, identical image and prompt, but with silent audio. Mouth stayed closed; motion energy was 3.5? lower than with real speech. Quantitative check supplemented with frame strips at quiet/loud audio moments.

7. **Email attachment timeout**  
   The SMTP client in `mxmail_v01.py` had a 30?second hard timeout, too short to upload the 6 MB MP3 report. Increased timeout to 60 seconds to get the email out reliably.

## GOTCHAS AND DEAD ENDS

- **Never symlink diffusion/audio models to green24.** The exFAT filesystem on that spinning disk caps reads at ~28?30 MB/s, making GPU rendering useless. Models must live on the NVMe.
- **Do not kill the dictation server** (PID ~94266, port 8123). It holds ~3.9 GB VRAM permanently. Work around it - the fp8 model fits with that headroom, nothing else needed.
- **GGUF quantization does not help** for this card. The useful quant (Q5_K_M) was still 15 GB, and model offloading to system RAM would kill speed. The NVMe move was the real fix.
- **The replacement computer's GPU (RTX 5060 8 GB) cannot hold Wan 2.2 S2V.** Any workflow that tries will OOM. This was the critical discovery that changed the cost?benefit of the existing Taygeta.
- **SMTP timeout** in `mxmail_v01.py` was raised to 60 s to accommodate ~6 MB attachments. Do not revert without testing large sends.
- **Workflow keys that matter:** `uni_pc` sampler, shift=8, cfg=6, 20 steps, sage attention on, `audio_scale=1.0`, 832?480 output, single 5?second chunk, tight framing. Using `euler` or multi?chunk >10 s will degrade quality/identity.
